# Audio Sources

Provenance registry for every audio file under `public/assets/`. Required by CLAUDE.md
"Build Guards & Asset Policy": no audio file may be added without a row here, and only
**CC0** or **self-generated** audio is allowed — an unresolved copyright claim on a sound
is one of the most common rejection reasons on Playables.

Lives at the repo root, not under `public/assets/`, deliberately: it's an internal
process document, not a game asset — it must never ship inside `dist/` or the submission
ZIP (see CLAUDE.md "Build Guards & Asset Policy").

**Most of this game's sound is not in this table at all**, and that is the design: `src/audio/sfx.ts`
renders six of its eight effects from arithmetic at boot, so they are self-generated by construction
and there is nothing to license. The two rows below are the deliberate exception — a coin and an
impact are *objects striking things*, which a waveform cannot be. The packs they came from are kept
under `dev-assets/audio/` (not shipped) along with `measure.py`, the instrument the picks were made
with.

## What was changed about the two CC0 files, and why

CC0 permits modification without saying so; this section exists because a provenance row that says
"unmodified" when the bytes differ is a row nobody can check twice.

- **Levelled into the existing mix.** Every generated sound bakes its own gain (the set runs
  0.24–0.55), so a recording shipped at full scale would arrive roughly twice as loud as everything
  around it. Each is scaled to the peak of the tone it replaced — the coin to `0.42` and the impact
  to `0.55` — so the balance the rest of the set was tuned against is unchanged. Measured after:
  0.42 and 0.53.
- **Downmixed to mono.** `impactGeneric_light_000` ships stereo, which for a one-shot effect doubles
  the decoded memory and buys nothing: nothing in this game pans a sound, and any positional cue
  would come from the game rather than from the file.
- **One lossy encode, from the original.** The first attempt reached the right level in three passes
  — mono, then volume, then a correction when the downmix turned out to have clipped and landed 27%
  hot. Both files are now built from the pack's own bytes in a single decode/scale/encode, which is
  the difference between two generations of Vorbis artefacts and four.

Delivered: `coin.ogg` 6362 bytes, `pickup.ogg` 6576 bytes, `hit.ogg` 7547 bytes — 20KB for the three.

## ⚠ The first pair was replaced after it shipped

They were `glass_001` (interface-sounds) and `impactGeneric_light_000` (impact-sounds), and both
cleared every number in `measure.py`. What no column carried is *genre*: a single struck partial at
99% tonality is the sound of hitting metal rather than of money, and a 64ms tick reads as thin.
Reported that way, and the replacements are picked on two properties the first round never measured
— **direction** (the coin rises, the damage falls) and **brightness at the sound's own base**
(3060Hz against 1959Hz). Both packs stay under `dev-assets/audio/` so the comparison can be redone
rather than taken on trust.
